5 rules from UC's own guide

4 of 8Answer any 4 of the 8 questions.
350Each answer can be up to 350 words.
0Favorite questions. No question helps or hurts you. They're numbered, not ranked.
NowMost of each answer should be about the present or recent past.
FactsReaders look at what you say about yourself, not your style or structure.

Good news: they're not grading your sentences.

Skip these

"I'm passionate about…" "Ever since I was little…" "It taught me so much." A dictionary definition A link (readers can't click it)

Source: University of California, Personal Insight Questions guide for counselors (fall 2025 first-year edition). Check UC's site for anything that changes.

What I saw as an admissions reader

The answers I remember were never the fanciest. They were the most specific: one real Saturday, one real number, one thing you actually did. UC says it out loud: they read for facts about you. So fill every blank with something only you could write.

— Ms Sweeney

Made-up student: Daniela, family business

In my aunt's taco truck on weekends, our card reader died on a Saturday with 20 people in line. I stepped up by taking cash, writing orders on paper plates and sending my cousin to the bank for change. The hard part was nobody put me in charge. My aunt was cooking and I'm 16. Because of it, we didn't lose one order. Now we keep a backup cash box and a printed price list. Now I train the 2 new weekend workers, and my robotics team calls me when a plan falls apart.

No title needed. Just a real Saturday.

The problem was my little brother would not practice reading. Creative doesn't have to mean art.

Made-up student: Ethan, lunch-line fixer

I noticed kids at my school skipped lunch because the line took 20 minutes. It bothered me because my friends were buying chips from the vending machine instead of eating. My part, not the group's, was timing the line for 2 weeks, making a chart and presenting it to our principal. What changed: they opened a second line in January, and the wait is about 8 minutes now. What I learned: numbers get adults to listen faster than complaints do.

Your part. Not just "we."
